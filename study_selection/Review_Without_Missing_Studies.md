# What the review looks like without the 20 unretrievable studies

**Situation:** 49 studies were sought. 29 full texts were retrieved. 20 could not be found despite a full search.
**Short answer:** the review stays intact. You lose **one trial that published meta-analyses used (Dickson 1991)**, and even that one can be included from its abstract. Everything else lost is either old, non-English with no abstract, or unlikely to be eligible. The retrieved set is still **larger than any existing topical-vs-oral meta-analysis**.

This matters more than your 20 unretrievable papers. Your workbook contains **registered topical-vs-oral or combination trials with no publication** (full list in `Download_List.csv`, "registry records"):

- **Combination designs with no results:** ChiCTR2100048260, IRCT2017092710222N14, IRCT2017103037093N1, IRCT20170929036477N2, IRCT20161204031229N7
- **Probably still ongoing (2023–2025):** ChiCTR2300075288, ChiCTR2400085612, ChiCTR2400092881, ChiCTR2400091706, ITMCTR2025000961

**Why it matters:** unpublished industry trials of a topical NSAID vs celecoxib are the classic setting for **non-reporting bias**, where unfavourable results never appear. Check each registry page for posted results. In GRADE, consider downgrading the topical-vs-oral comparison one level for publication bias, or at least discuss it. The five 2023–2025 trials go in a "Characteristics of ongoing studies" table.

## 5. How to report it

**PRISMA 2020 flow (full-text part):**

```
Reports sought for retrieval ................. 49
Reports not retrieved ........................ 14  → "studies awaiting classification" table
Reports assessed for eligibility ............. 35  (29 full texts + 6 on abstract only†)
   Excluded, with reasons .................... 2 on abstract + [those excluded at full-text screening]
Studies included in review ................... [after full-text screening]
   of which in meta-analysis ................. [after data extraction]
Registers: registered trials with no results . 22 records → ongoing / unpublished table
```
†Dickson 1991, Takagishi 1985, Özgen 2011 (conference abstract), Dehghan 2018, Bolten 1994, De Boer 2011: full text unavailable, eligibility judged on the abstract. Separately, 265 ANCHORED records were removed by protocol amendment before retrieval.

**Ready-to-paste limitation paragraph:**

> Despite searching libraries, interlibrary loan and publisher archives, full texts of 20 potentially relevant reports could not be obtained. Most were Japanese, German or Spanish publications from 1980–1997. For six, the abstract was sufficient to judge eligibility: four were included (one only as a conference abstract) and contributed narratively, with dichotomous data where the abstract reported them; two were excluded. Fourteen are listed as studies awaiting classification. The largest unavailable trial (Dickson 1991, n = 235) and both unavailable Japanese trials with known results reported no difference between topical and oral NSAIDs, consistent with the pooled estimate. We therefore consider it unlikely that the unavailable reports would change our conclusions. Several registered head-to-head and combination trials, including industry-sponsored trials completed more than 15 years ago, have no publicly available results. This raises the possibility of non-reporting bias, which we considered in our GRADE assessment.

**Sensitivity analyses to pre-specify** (add to the protocol amendment):
1. Excluding studies with data from abstracts only (Takagishi 1985, and Rother 2013 if you use abstract data).
2. Excluding conference abstracts (Rother 2013, Özgen 2011).
3. Excluding studies at high risk of bias (for example the open-label trials Doi 2010, Shinde 2017, Underwood 2008 and Sun 2026; confirm blinding for the others at full text).
